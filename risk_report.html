Reading this: VaR is the loss threshold that, at the 95% confidence level, we do not expect to exceed over 30 trading days — i.e. there is a 5% chance of a worse outcome. CVaR (Expected Shortfall) is the average loss in those worst 5% of outcomes, always higher than VaR. The diversification benefit is the reduction in portfolio VaR versus simply summing each metal's individual VaR, reflecting the fact that not all prices crash simultaneously. Parametric and EWMA differ slightly here because current market volatility is close to the 5-year average; in volatile periods the EWMA figure will be materially higher.

Known Limitations & Future Work
ItemStatus
LME Nickel live feed (stainless proxy)Synthetic GBM fallback in use — NI=F data unavailable via Yahoo Finance. Stainless risk metrics are model-generated, not market-derived.
Sell-side transaction dataNot yet ingested — only buy-side data modelled. Realised P&L tracking requires sale records.
Holding costs (storage, labour, insurance)Not modelled — these costs reduce true P&L and should be deducted from unrealised gain.
Basis spread riskFixed basis assumed — real basis fluctuates with supply/demand and grade mix. A 5-point basis compression on copper ICW wire costs ~$58K at current exposure.
Zinc as independent brass driverBrass proxied by copper only. Zinc (~30% of yellow brass alloy) is not independently modelled.
Adjustments column in CSVAssumed that the "Cost" column is inclusive of adjustments. If adjustments are additive, purchase costs for some lots may be understated.
Seasonal demand patternsNot incorporated. Scrap demand and pricing are seasonally affected (auto, construction cycles).
√T horizon scalingParametric and EWMA VaR use √T rule, which assumes independent daily returns. Volatility clustering in metals means this may understate multi-day VaR in crisis periods.
